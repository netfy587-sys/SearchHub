AN INDEPENDENT, PLAIN-LANGUAGE GUIDE

How basketball actually works

From five-player lineups to the three-point arc — the rules, positions, and vocabulary of the game, explained without assuming you already follow it.

GAME STRUCTURE

A game is played in four parts

Most professional leagues split the game into four quarters, though the exact length varies slightly between competitions.

Q1

Opening quarter

Teams feel out pace, matchups, and early foul calls before settling into a rhythm.

Q2

Building a lead

Bench rotations get heavier use here, testing depth beyond the starting five.

Q3

Coming out of the break

Halftime adjustments show up here, often the highest-scoring quarter of the game.

Q4

Closing time

Fouls are used deliberately to stop the clock, and star players see the most minutes.

POSITIONS

Five players, five different jobs

1

Point guard

Brings the ball up and usually runs the offence, deciding when to pass, drive, or shoot.

2

Shooting guard

Typically the team's most reliable outside shooter, moving without the ball to get open.

3

Small forward

A flexible role that can score close to the basket or from outside, and often guards the opponent's best scorer.

4

Power forward

Plays closer to the basket, contesting rebounds and finishing plays near the rim.

5

Centre

Usually the tallest player, anchoring defence near the basket and controlling rebounds.

SCORING

Not every basket is worth the same

1

Free throw

An unguarded shot from the foul line, awarded after certain fouls — worth a single point.

2

Field goal

Any basket made from inside the three-point line during regular play.

3

Three-pointer

A shot made from beyond the arc marked on the court — the same shot is worth more the farther back it's taken from.

SHOT TYPES

How the ball actually gets to the hoop

Drive and layup

A player moves toward the basket and lays the ball in off the backboard or through the rim at close range.

Jump shot

The shooter releases the ball at the peak of a jump, the standard method for mid-range and three-point attempts.

Free throw

Taken from a fixed line after certain fouls, with no defender allowed to contest it.

THE COURT

The lines mean something

Every marking on a basketball court exists to answer a rules question — where a shot counts for more, how long an offensive player can stand near the basket, and where a throw-in happens after the ball goes out of bounds.

Court sizes vary slightly between leagues, but the core layout — a centre circle, a key near each basket, and a three-point arc — stays recognisable everywhere the game is played.

MILESTONES

A short history of the game

1891

Invented with a peach basket

James Naismith devised the original game using a soccer ball and two peach baskets nailed to a gymnasium balcony.

1936

Olympic debut

Basketball was played at the Summer Olympics for the first time, at the Berlin Games.

1946

A major professional league forms

The Basketball Association of America was founded, later merging to become one of the sport's biggest professional leagues.

1979

The three-point line is adopted

The three-point shot, first tried in smaller leagues, was adopted at the top level of professional basketball.

1992

Professionals allowed at the Olympics

Olympic eligibility rules changed to allow professional players to compete for their national teams.
